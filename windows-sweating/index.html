    How it works: the limits come from the University of Minnesota's cold-climate table (W. J. Angell, "Indoor Winter Relative Humidity: What Is Acceptable?", Minnesota Extension Service, 1988), measured at 70°F inside with a 15 mph wind. The tool turns that table into a glass-surface temperature, so it still works when your thermostat isn't at 70. Deep window wells, closed blinds and heavy curtains keep warm air off the glass and make it sweat sooner.
